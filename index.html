Sector 01

Law Firms

Legal work produces some of the most carefully reasoned text in any profession. Where a firm holds material it has the right to use, and where client confidentiality and privilege can be fully protected, that material may be of interest to developers of legal AI.

Material we discuss

  • Firm-authored precedents and template libraries
  • Historical case materials cleared for use
  • Know-how notes and practice guides
  • Structured matter and clause data

What we look at first

  • Ownership and client consent position
  • Privilege and confidentiality exposure
  • Anonymisation feasibility
  • Partnership and risk committee appetite

Sector 02

Asset managers, research houses and advisory firms hold deep archives of analysis and decision records. Historical research and workflow data can help AI developers build tools that reason about markets and businesses.

Material we discuss

  • Long-run business and sector records

What we look at first

  • Age and relevance of the material
  • Compliance function sign-off

Our approach

Qualification, introduction, evaluation.

A four-stage process designed so that you stay in control. Each stage needs your agreement before the next one begins, and you can stop at any point.

  1. 01

    Initial conversation

    You and Meridian Data

    A confidential discussion with a senior decision-maker. We learn about your organisation, the kinds of data you hold and what you would want from a partnership. We do not ask for any data at this stage.

  2. 02

    Preliminary qualification

    Meridian Data, with your input

    We work through a short set of questions to decide whether the opportunity warrants further discussion.

    • Do you own or have the right to license the material?
    • Are client, third-party or personal data interests involved?
    • Is the material of a type and volume AI developers seek?
    • Is there internal sponsorship to take it further?

    If the answer is no, we say so plainly.

  3. 03

    Introduction

    With your written permission

    Where the opportunity qualifies, and only with your permission, we introduce your organisation to a suitable AI partner. Introductions are made on a descriptive basis, under confidentiality terms, and without disclosing the underlying data.

  4. 04

    Commercial evaluation

    You and the AI partner, independently

    The parties assess the data, the applicable permissions and potential next steps directly with each other, supported by their own legal and technical advisers. Any agreement is between you and the partner.

Our role

We make the introduction. You make the decision.

Meridian Data acts as an intermediary. We do not take custody of client data, we do not negotiate on your behalf without instruction, and we do not replace your legal, compliance or information security advisers. Our job is to identify credible opportunities and put the right people in the room.

Responsible data

Confidentiality, permissions and governance.

Data partnerships only work when every party can stand behind them. These are the standards we apply before any opportunity moves forward.

Confidentiality

Every conversation with us is treated as confidential from the outset.

  • Mutual confidentiality terms before any detailed discussion
  • Your identity is not shared with prospective partners without consent
  • We work from descriptions of data, never the data itself
  • Need-to-know access within our team

Permissions

An opportunity only exists where the right to use the material is clear.

  • Ownership and licensing rights confirmed early
  • Client, customer and third-party interests identified
  • Professional duties, including legal professional privilege, respected
  • Lawful basis for any personal data assessed under UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018

Governance

Partnerships should fit within your existing governance, not work around it.

  • Your risk, compliance and data protection functions involved
  • Anonymisation and data minimisation considered first
  • Clear limits on how a partner may use the data
  • Documented decisions at each stage

Our boundaries

What we will not do.

  • Introduce an opportunity where the data owner cannot show the right to use the material.
  • Receive, store or transfer client data on anyone's behalf.
  • Share your organisation's name with a prospective partner before you agree.
  • Encourage the disclosure of privileged or confidential client information.
  • Give legal advice. We recommend that every party takes independent legal advice before any agreement.
